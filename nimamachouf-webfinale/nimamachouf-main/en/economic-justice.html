Economic justice

Canada is a wealthy country. The question is who that wealth benefits. Nimâ wants those who have the most to pay their fair share, and working people never to be left behind.

  1. Commitment 1

    Make the ultra-rich pay their fair share

    While the cost of living soars, the largest fortunes keep growing. Truly progressive taxation can fund housing, health care and the green transition without placing a heavier burden on families.

    What we propose

    We propose an annual 1% tax on fortunes over $20 million, and an 80% tax rate on the portion of personal income above one million dollars a year. We also need to restore the corporate tax rate to 18%, tax excess profits made during pandemics and disasters, and raise taxes on companies that maintain extreme pay gaps between executives and employees.

    We need to close tax loopholes, get serious about tax evasion and tax havens, and finally make web giants pay their fair share.

  2. Commitment 2

    A just transition that leaves no one behind

    The energy transition must create good jobs, not destroy them. Workers in affected sectors must be supported, trained and helped into the jobs of tomorrow.

    What we propose

    We need to invest in just transition programs in sectors facing layoffs, and give real support to workers moving into a cleaner energy economy.

    That means a worker-centred industrial strategy: when public money supports a company, it must create good, unionized jobs here. We also want a “Buy Clean” policy that favours low-carbon steel, aluminum, cement and wood made in Canada.

  3. Commitment 3

    Public ownership to end price gouging

    Cell plans, internet, bank fees, credit cards: too many sectors are dominated by a few big companies that set prices as they please. When the private sector gouges, the public sector must offer another option.

    What we propose

    We propose creating a public telecommunications Crown corporation to offer affordable internet and cell service across the country, and restoring basic public banking services through Canada Post.

    At the same time, we must stop excessive cable, cell phone and bank fees, cap exorbitant credit card interest rates and ATM fees, and give the Competition Bureau more teeth in banking, energy, telecommunications, air travel and pharmaceuticals.

Let’s demand better, together.
