Housing

In Laurier—Sainte-Marie, as across the country, housing costs too much. The market alone won’t fix the crisis. Nimâ wants the federal government to become a real builder of homes for people again.

  1. Commitment 1

    Make up for 30 years of underinvestment in public housing

    Since the 1990s, Ottawa has largely pulled out of building public housing. We need a permanent federal program that matches the scale of the crisis.

    What we propose

    We propose a permanent federal public housing program to build 4.5 million non-market homes over ten years: homes that are truly affordable, accessible and energy-efficient. The goal: 20% of the housing stock outside the market.

    This effort must be part of a national strategy guaranteeing everyone safe, affordable housing, carried out with the provinces, municipalities, Indigenous communities and housing organizations. It must also include stable funding for housing designed by and for Indigenous peoples.

  2. Commitment 2

    Build social, co-op and non-profit housing

    Co-ops, non-profits and social housing keep rents affordable for good. These models have proven themselves, including right here in Montreal.

    What we propose

    We propose to develop, fund and preserve social, co-op and non-profit housing, working with every level of government. These homes must be adapted for people living with different types of disabilities.

    We also support helping low-income households make their homes more energy-efficient, which lowers their bills.

  3. Commitment 3

    Build homes for everyone, not profits for developers

    A home is first and foremost a place to live, not a financial product. We need to curb speculation, strengthen tenants’ rights and protect people at risk of losing their homes.

    What we propose

    We propose to end real estate speculation and the tax advantages of real estate investment trusts (REITs), and to strengthen tenants’ rights across the country.

    We want a homelessness prevention fund that provides emergency rent assistance to people facing eviction, and an expanded “Housing First” approach that combines permanent housing with mental health, addiction and employment services. We must also stop dismantling encampments on federal land and help the people living there find permanent homes.

Let’s demand better, together.

These commitments won’t happen on their own. Join Nimâ’s campaign in Laurier—Sainte-Marie.
